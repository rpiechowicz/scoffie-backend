### Features vs plain StoreKit 2

- **Paywalls**: remote-configured native paywalls (templates, custom, "AI Editor"), no app update needed; iOS 15+, Android 7+, macOS 12+, web; not watchOS/tvOS/visionOS; unlimited Offerings/Paywalls per Experiments variant. Minimum purchases-ios 5.27.1 (multipage needs newer). https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/paywalls (undated, fetched 2026-08-27).
- **Customer Center**: self-service cancel flow, refund requests (iOS only), plan changes (iOS only), automatic promo offers to save cancellations, cancellation-reason survey, virtual-currency balances. Docs say "available on Pro and Enterprise plans" — note that "Pro" is the plan that is $0 under $2.5k MTR, so this reads as included for a small app; third-party listings also say Paywalls are on the free tier (https://www.spotsaas.com/product/revenuecat/pricing, https://www.saasworthy.com/product/revenuecat/pricing — low-reliability). Confirm in dashboard. https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/customer-center (undated, fetched 2026-08-27).
- **Experiments (A/B/n)**, 40+ metrics, MMP integrations, web-to-app funnels, lifecycle triggers: listed as included in Pro on the pricing page.
- **Virtual Currency** (credits/tokens balance, currency packs, subscription-granted bonuses; explicitly pitched for AI credits: "an AI image generator might give free users some credits each day and sell packs of credits for heavy users"), included from the free tier. https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/revenuecat-virtual-currency (2025-08-05, updated 2025-08-13).
- **Webhooks — can they gate backend features? Yes, this is the documented pattern.**
  - Event types: INITIAL*PURCHASE, RENEWAL, CANCELLATION, UNCANCELLATION, NON_RENEWING_PURCHASE, SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSED, EXPIRATION, BILLING_ISSUE, PRODUCT_CHANGE, SUBSCRIPTION_EXTENDED, REFUND_REVERSED, INVOICE_ISSUANCE, TRANSFER, TEMPORARY_ENTITLEMENT_GRANT, VIRTUAL_CURRENCY_TRANSACTION, EXPERIMENT_ENROLLMENT, PURCHASE_REDEEMED, PRICE_INCREASE_CONSENT*\*; lifecycle events carry `app_user_id`, `product_id`, `entitlement_ids` (may be null if product not mapped), `expiration_at_ms`, `period_type` (TRIAL/NORMAL…). https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks/event-types-and-fields (undated, fetched 2026-08-27).
  - Delivery: custom Authorization header plus HMAC-SHA256 `X-RevenueCat-Webhook-Signature`; 5 retries at 5/10/20/40/80 min; RC recommends calling `GET /subscribers` after any webhook rather than trusting the payload alone. https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks (undated, fetched 2026-08-27).
  - Recommended architecture: your backend persists entitlements from webhooks; your DB is the source of truth; app asks your backend before unlocking. https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/guides/common-architecture (undated, fetched 2026-08-27).
  - Worked example for gating server-side/agentic features (RevenueCat-authored gist, 2026-03-08): store `user_id, product_id, status, expires_at_ms`; unlock on INITIAL_PURCHASE/RENEWAL, revoke on EXPIRATION not CANCELLATION ("a canceled subscription is still active until its expiration_at_ms"); dedupe by event `id`; respond within 60 s; fall back to `GET /v1/subscribers/{id}` for high-stakes calls; handle `logIn()` alias merges. https://gist.github.com/revenuecatclaw/dd0376e82b7d1077fb86716cbb05d63d
- **StoreKit 2 alone gives**: JWS-signed transactions verifiable server-side, `Transaction.currentEntitlements`, App Store Server Notifications V2 (your own webhook endpoint), `SubscriptionStoreView` (iOS 17+), offer codes; RC adds cross-platform entitlements, server-side validation done for you, normalized webhooks with retries, analytics (MRR/churn/LTV/trial conversion), remote Offerings, Paywalls, Experiments. Third-party comparison: https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/storekit-2-vs-revenuecat-ios-subscriptions (2026-03-15, updated May 2026) and https://vmobify.com/blog/storekit-vs-revenuecat. (That first article's "$120/month minimum at $9,990 MTR" conflicts with the 1% rule; treat as unverified.)

## 2. Apple: commission, EU/Poland terms, PLN price points, VAT, App Review guidelines

### Commission

- Standard 30%; auto-renewable subscriptions drop to 15% after one year of paid service (12 months of paid renewals); **Small Business Program: 15%** if proceeds (net of Apple's commission) across all associated accounts were ≤ $1M USD in the previous calendar year; new developers qualify; must be Account Holder, accept latest Paid Apps agreement (Schedule 2), list Associated Developer Accounts; reduced rate starts 15 days after end of the fiscal month of approval; exceeding $1M mid-year moves future sales to standard rate. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/ (undated, fetched 2026-08-27). Third-party guides: https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/apple-app-store-small-business-program-guide (2026), https://adapty.io/blog/app-store-small-business-program/ (2026), RC doc: https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/platform-resources/apple-platform-resources/app-store-small-business-program.

### EU (Poland is in scope) — new unified terms announced 2026-08-18, effective 2026-10-01

Source: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/ (2026-08-18) and https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/ (fetched 2026-08-27).

- "For App Store apps using Apple In-App Purchase, the commission will be 26 percent." "For the vast majority of developers, including those in the App Store Small Business Program … and for auto-renewing subscriptions after their first year, it will be 15 percent."
- Alternative payment processing inside App Store apps: 20% (10% for SBP etc.); link-out to web checkout: 15% (10% for SBP); apps distributed outside the App Store: 5% Core Technology Commission. Core Technology Fee (€0.50/install), Initial Acquisition Fee and Store Services Fee are eliminated. Chosen payment options must be kept for 12 months.
- Opt-in, not automatic: "To move to the unified EU terms, they will need to agree to the updated Apple Developer Program License Agreement… subject to the unified EU terms starting October 1, 2026, or the date they agree, whichever is later." Old Alternative Terms Addendum / External Purchase Link addendum are superseded by DPLA Attachment 14 (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gmws0jgp, 2026-08-18).
- Coverage: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/apple-overhauls-its-eu-app-store-fees-loosens-rules-for-alternative-app-stores/ (2026-08-18); https://blog.funnelfox.com/apple-app-store-fees-2026-eu-dma/ (2026-08-25) — notes the layered model it replaces had been in effect since Jan 2026.
- **Practical relevance for a Polish solo dev under $1M using Apple IAP only:** commission is 15% under either the classic terms (SBP) or the unified EU terms; the unified terms only matter if you want in-app alternative payments (10%) or link-out (10%) — and those come with your own VAT/payment-processing obligations (see VAT below). The older SBP page still mentions a 10% year-2 rate "for developers on alternative EU terms" (legacy addendum being phased out).

### VAT

- Apple is merchant of record; charges VAT to the buyer; "Developer Proceeds = App Price − VAT/Tax − Apple Commission" (example: $10 price, 20% VAT → $2.00; 30% of remainder → $2.40; proceeds $5.60). Apple collects/remits in regions listed in Exhibit B of the Paid Apps agreement (EU included). If you use EU alternative payment options you must provide an EU VAT ID and handle VAT yourself. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/making-payments-to-apple/understanding-taxes/ (fetched 2026-08-27).
- Developer's supply to Apple Distribution International (Cork, Ireland; VAT ID IE9700053D) is a B2B reverse-charge, 0% VAT; worked example €85.71 incl. 20% VAT → €71.43 net → 70% = €50 to developer. https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/71030 (posts 2017-01 to 2020-11). Apple confirms "proceeds… calculated based on the tax-exclusive price" when it collects VAT: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=e1b1hcmv (2022-09-19).
- Poland's standard VAT rate (23%) is not separately sourced in this research; apply it to the formula above (e.g. 29,99 zł → 24,38 zł net of VAT → 20,72 zł at 15%).

### App Review guidelines relevant to an AI chat assistant

Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (fetched 2026-08-27; latest changes 2026-06-08 per https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=a233fmpw).

- **5.1.2(i)** (clarified 2025-11-13): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl (2025-11-13); coverage https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai. Practitioner guidance: in-app disclosure naming the provider and purpose before the first call; a privacy-policy line or bundled ToS checkbox is not enough; on-device (Foundation Models/Core ML) is exempt. https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-06-apple-ai-consent-rule-5-1-2-i-implementation-guide (2026-05-06), https://dev.to/arshtechpro/apples-guideline-512i-the-ai-data-sharing-rule-that-will-impact-every-ios-developer-1b0p.
- **5.1.1** privacy policy link in metadata and in-app; consent for data collection; paid functionality may not depend on granting data access (ii); data minimization (iii); account deletion in-app if you offer accounts (v); sensitive-service apps should be submitted by a legal entity (ix).
- **5.1.3** health/fitness data may not be used for marketing/data mining; disclose specific health data collected; no PHI in iCloud.
- **1.4.1** medical apps: "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor… before making medical decisions" — relevant if the assistant gives dietary advice.
- **1.2** UGC: filtering of objectionable material, report mechanism with timely responses, block abusive users, published contact info (new paragraph on developer responsibility added 2026-06-08: https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-against-apps-that-do-not-add-value-to-the-app-store/). **4.7 / 4.7.1** explicitly names "chatbots" and requires the same filter/report/block controls and 3.1 compliance; **4.7.5** age-restriction mechanism for content exceeding the app rating.
- **3.1.1** subscriptions/feature unlocks must use IAP; "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire" (important if you sell AI credit packs); restore mechanism required. **3.1.2(a)** ongoing value, period ≥ 7 days, works on all user devices, "Subscriptions may include consumable credits"; **3.1.2(b)** seamless upgrade/downgrade; **3.1.2(c)** state clearly what the user gets ("How many issues per month? How much cloud storage?") — i.e. state AI message caps on the paywall.
- **4.2** minimum functionality; **4.3(b)** (2026-06-08) low-value/copycat apps may be removed.
- **2.3.6** answer age-rating questions honestly. **Age-rating overhaul**: 13+/16+/18+ added, 12+/17+ removed; new questions on in-app controls, capabilities, medical/wellness, violence; "You must consider how all app features, including AI assistants and chatbot functionality, impact the frequency of sensitive content"; deadline to answer 2026-01-31. https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf (2025-07-24); https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/24/apple-notifies-developers-of-new-app-store-age-rating-system/. Observed ratings: Samsung Food 18+, MyFitnessPal 16+, Fitatu 13+, Yazio 9+ (PL App Store, 2026-08-27).
- DPLA (not the guidelines) gained an AI/ML section 3.3.11 incl. Foundation Models terms on 2026-06-08 (https://applemagazine.com/apple-developer-program-license-agreement-wwdc26/).

## 4. Conversion / churn / ARPU benchmarks

### RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115k+ apps, $16B, data mostly 2025) — https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps (published 2026-03-19; summary blog https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026 updated 2026-04-22)

- Trial-to-paid median: 29.7% global; Western Europe 29.7%; **Health & Fitness 37.7%**; by trial length: ≤4 days 25.5%, 5–9 days 37.4%, 17–32 days 42.5%. 54% of H&F apps use 5–9-day trials.
- Download-to-paid (D35): 2.0% overall; **H&F 2.9%**; freemium 2.1% vs hard paywall 10.7%; low-priced 1.4%, mid 2.0%, high 2.8%.
- Revenue per install: D14 H&F $0.48 (all $0.23); D60 H&F $0.66 (all $0.34); hard paywall D60 $3.09 vs freemium $0.38.
- Realized LTV per payer after 1 year: H&F $35.64; global $23; mid-priced apps $15.78 after month 1.
- Prices: median weekly $5.00, monthly $8–10, annual $34.80; H&F annual median $39.94. 68% of H&F apps sell annual plans; annual = 60.6% of H&F revenue.
- Churn: month-1 = 35% of all annual-plan cancellations; year-1 annual cancellation ~72%; 3-day trials: 55.4% cancel on day 0. Billing-failure share of cancellations: App Store 14%, Google Play 31%.

## 5. LLM cost per active user and cost-control patterns

- **RevenueCat, "The hidden cost of AI features"** (2026-03-26, upd. 2026-04-17): worked examples of $0.02 per AI-active user/month (10 requests × 1k tokens on a cheap model) and $0.10 with heavier use; at $6 ARPU an AI cost of $0.18 = 3% of revenue, at $3.50 ARPU $0.60 = 17%; 45k AI-active users → $900–$4,500/month. Recommended: model routing, cache/reuse outputs (regeneration is 20%+ of cost), daily/monthly caps and one-time trial allowances, gate heavy AI behind higher tiers, prefer variable-cost APIs over self-hosting. https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-feature-cost-subscription-app-margins
- **Inworld, "Unit economics of consumer AI apps"** (2026-07-07): text chat app at 20 active days × 50 messages (600 in / 150 out tokens each): **$0.09 to $7.50 per DAU per month** depending on model (81× spread); voice $2.58–$18.24; with ~3% paid conversion each payer must carry ~33 free users' AI cost; levers: model tiering, prefix caching, volume discounts, "small-first" routing with escalation. https://inworld.ai/resources/unit-economics-of-consumer-ai-apps
- **FinOps LLM benchmarks** (July 2026, third-party): customer-facing chatbot $0.10 (1k conversations/mo) to $5–11 (100k/mo) per concurrent user; prompt caching ~90% cheaper on cached tokens; batch API 50% off. https://finopsllm.com/research/llm-cost-per-user-benchmarks
- **Indie meal-planner case (MealestroAI, Medium)**: GPT-4o; a full personalized weekly plan (7 dinners + images + batch-prep tips, 23+ API calls) ≈ **$0.70 per user per week**; shopping-list generation ≈ $0.03; earlier one-plan-for-15-subscribers version cost < $0.30/week total. https://medium.com/@dgitalizeme/mealestroai-smart-meal-planner-7e4c8ff5a93c (page blocked on fetch; figures from search index, date not retrievable).
- **Production cost guide** (2026-05-10): support-chat request with 2,050 input tokens at $3/M ≈ $0.006 input cost; 100k sessions × 5 turns ≈ $3,075/month input-only; compress system prompt 900→200 tokens, truncate history to 6–8 exchanges, set `max_tokens`, exact-match cache (24 h), per-feature cost logging — "cut most teams' LLM bills by 40–60%". https://blog.codercops.com/blog/llm-api-cost-optimization-production-2026
- **Why providers cap usage** (2025-08-13): GitHub Copilot ~$30 compute vs $10 fee (heavy users up to $80); patterns: daily/weekly caps, tokens-per-day caps, degradation to cheaper model when exceeded, tiered access, overage/pay-per-use extensions, abuse monitoring. https://www.getmonetizely.com/blogs/why-llm-providers-are-capping-usage
- **Margins**: heavy ChatGPT Plus users' inference exceeds the $20 fee (https://beyond10x.substack.com/p/the-math-behind-your-20-subscription, 2026-06-06); app-layer AI companies ~25% gross margin early ("supernovas") vs ~60% for steadier ones; model providers ~50–60% (https://www.tanayj.com/p/the-gross-margin-debate-in-ai, 2025-09-02).
- **Common cost-control patterns seen across sources**: (a) hard or soft monthly/rolling-window caps with pause-then-reset (Notion, Grammarly, pre-Aug-2026 ChatGPT); (b) degradation to a cheaper model instead of blocking (ChatGPT Free fallback to mini model); (c) prepaid credit packs / virtual currency for overage (Notion credits, Canva AI Pass, RevenueCat Virtual Currency — note Apple 3.1.1: purchased credits may not expire); (d) separate AI tier above base premium (Fitatu Premium+AI, MFP Premium+); (e) trial allowances measured in generations rather than days; (f) engineering levers — routing, prompt caching, history truncation, output caps, caching repeated results.
